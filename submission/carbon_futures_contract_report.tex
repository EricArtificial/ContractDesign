\section{Underlying Asset and Trading Product}
% S01--S06
This proposal selects China Emission Allowances (CEA) as the underlying asset for a Guangzhou Futures Exchange (GFEX) carbon futures contract, focusing on the allowance procurement risk of covered entities. In 2025, the national carbon market covered 3,378 entities in power generation, steel, cement, and aluminum smelting\cite{mee2026}, providing a broad base of potential hedgers. China Certified Emission Reductions (CCER) can also be used for compliance offsetting\cite{ccer2023}, making them a relevant alternative underlying. We therefore compare CEA and CCER in terms of standardization, price risk, and potential hedging demand.

\subsection{Suitability for Standardization}
% S07--S10

CEA is easier to standardize because it has fewer asset-level differences and a common market price reference. Allowance eligibility mainly depends on vintage, registration status, validity for compliance, and transferability\cite{mee2021reg,mee2024carryover}. CCER additionally differs by project, methodology, generation year, and registration batch\cite{ccer2023}. The national registry identifies CEA ownership and status under common rules\cite{mee2021reg}, making delivery grades easier to define by vintage and usable status.

The Shanghai Environment and Energy Exchange publishes composite CEA prices\cite{she2025daily}, providing a common spot reference for futures pricing. CCER prices vary across projects: in 2025, average transaction prices were CNY 81.58 per tonne for grid-connected solar thermal projects and CNY 69.27 for grid-connected offshore wind projects\cite{ccer2025annual}. Changes in the project mix therefore affect the cross-project average.

\subsection{Price Volatility}
% S11--S16
CEA price movements directly affect compliance procurement costs for firms with allowance deficits, creating a clear hedging need. In 2025, composite CEA prices ranged from CNY 50.34 to 97.01 per tonne; the year-end closing price fell 23.45\% from the previous year-end\cite{she2025annual}. Over the common trading window, the standard deviation of daily price changes was CNY 1.07 per tonne (Table~\ref{tab:underlying-liquidity}). Procurement risk is therefore observable and measurable.

Figure~\ref{fig:underlying-liquidity}(a) shows frequent sharp CCER price movements over short periods. Such volatility could increase margin and cash-management costs for hedgers and reduce hedge effectiveness. Panels (b) and (c) also show concentrated CCER trading: the ten largest trading days account for 65.40\% of sample volume, indicating less continuous spot-market price formation.

\subsection{Potential Hedging Demand}
% S17--S23
Potential demand for long hedges comes from firms that need to purchase additional allowances before surrender. In the first compliance cycle (2019--2020), 847 firms, or 39.18\% of covered entities, had a combined shortfall of about 188 million tonnes\cite{mee2022compliance}. A recent disclosure provides a concrete example: after accounting for its existing allowance balance, Changyuan Electric Power estimated a shortfall of 511,200 tonnes for 2025 compliance and approved purchases in 2026\cite{changyuan2026purchase}. A CNY 10 per tonne price increase would raise its procurement cost by CNY 5.112 million. Deficit firms thus have an economic incentive to lock in CEA purchase prices.
Procurement risk is also seasonal. Q4 accounted for 79\% of annual CEA volume in 2024\cite{mee2025development} and 58.18\% in 2025\cite{she2025daily}. Concentrated year-end trading strengthens the case for managing procurement prices before the surrender deadline.

\subsection{Underlying and Deliverable Allowances}
% S24--S31
The benchmark deliverable is CEA for the emissions year subject to surrender in the delivery year, with the benchmark vintage fixed when the contract is listed. For example, surrender in 2026 covers 2025 emissions, so the benchmark is 2025 CEA\cite{mee2026work}. Other vintages are eligible only if they have equivalent validity for the same compliance year under MEE rules and have completed any required carry-over registration. The rules currently permit 2026 allowances for 2025 surrender\cite{mee2026allocation}; 2019--2024 allowances qualify only after recall and reissue as 2025 allowances\cite{mee2024carryover,mee2026allocation}.

Only allowances registered in an active national carbon registry account and available for transfer qualify for delivery\cite{mee2021reg}. Surrendered, canceled, frozen, pledged, or otherwise transfer-restricted allowances are excluded.

Benchmark allowances and eligible substitutes may be combined for delivery on a tonne-for-tonne basis, with each vintage verified for validity in the same compliance year. If policy changes affect eligibility, GFEX should update the eligible list and require replacement of ineligible allowances under preannounced contingency rules.

\section{Proposed Contract Specifications}
% S32--S35
The proposed trading unit is 1,000 tonnes of CO\textsubscript{2} equivalent per contract. ICE Endex EUA futures also cover 1,000 allowances, each representing one tonne of CO\textsubscript{2} equivalent\cite{iceEuaSpecs}. Domestic purchases can reach 100,000 tonnes: Sinopec bought that quantity of CEA from China Resources Group on July 21, 2021\cite{sinopec2021bulk}, equivalent to 100 proposed contracts. Rounding to the nearest whole contract leaves at most 500 tonnes unmatched, or 0.5\% of that exposure. This size balances hedge precision and the value of an individual contract. Table~\ref{tab:terms} sets out the proposed specifications.

\section{Delivery, Reference Prices, and Settlement Prices}
% S63--S68: unresolved source instructions remain visible draft fields.
\noindent\textbf{Method and rationale.} \pending{compare physical delivery and cash settlement; select the method and prerequisites.} Physical delivery requires rules on eligible assets and accounts, freezing or transfer of allowances, payment, delivery failure, and the verification status of registry interfaces. Cash settlement requires index constituents, transaction types, data licensing, an observation window, a weighted calculation formula, and manipulation safeguards.

\noindent\textbf{Daily settlement price.} \pending{futures data source, calculation window and formula, publication time, and no-trade or disruption rules.}

\noindent\textbf{Final settlement price.} \pending{source, formula, observation window, publication time, corrections, and fallbacks for the delivery price or final cash settlement price.} Explain convergence with spot prices. Distinguish the spot closing price, daily futures settlement price, and final settlement price.
